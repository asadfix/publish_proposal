\section{Step 6: The Cost of Assuming Equivalence, in Simulation}
\label{sec:cost}

The final step quantifies the cost of assuming equivalence, an assumption the field currently makes without testing it. Unlike Steps 1 to 5, it does not require the corpus at all, because the ground truth is constructed: we simulate platforms whose measurement properties are known exactly, and ask how badly the standard index construction misreads them. The naive index pools posts across platforms, the construction canonical since \citet{oconnor2010}; the dissertation itself built such an index (Figure~\ref{fig:naive}), and its decomposition (Figure~\ref{fig:seasonal}) shows the trend and seasonal components an analyst would report from it.

\paragraph{Design.}
A latent daily sentiment series carries a known negative shift at an anchor event and recovers gradually. Each platform observes the same series through its own measurement function: an affordance bias shifts every expression, platform-specific thresholds discretise the result into negative, neutral, and positive, and expression noise is added; platform volume shares also drift over time, so composition changes as it did in the post-API record \citep{davidson2023, goanta2026}. Two indices are then computed daily. The naive index is the pooled mean of coded values across platforms. The adjusted index estimates each platform's latent value by inverting that platform's measurement function, then pools the estimates with inverse-variance weights. In the application the measurement parameters come from Step 3; in the simulation they are known, so the exercise isolates what adjustment can recover in principle and what pooling destroys.

\paragraph{Quantities reported.}
We compare the two indices on what an analyst would actually report: the estimated sentiment shift at the event, the day-by-day sign of aggregate sentiment, and the recovery trajectory, over repeated draws with fixed seeds. \todo{Final generating parameters (platform biases, thresholds, noise, composition drift, event shape, replication count) to be pre-registered with EH; insert the resulting numbers here.} A prototype run with illustrative parameters (Figure~\ref{fig:simdemo}) shows the pattern the design is built to expose: the pooled index misstates the size of the event shift and disagrees with the true sign of aggregate sentiment on a sixth of simulated days, while the adjusted index tracks the truth closely. The simulation code, with its seed, ships with the paper.

\paragraph{What is deferred to the application.}
Corpus-based variants of this exercise, reweighting platform shares to mimic a platform losing API access, and the forecast-error comparison that feeds both indices to a Prophet model as the dissertation's forecasting stage did, require the re-collected corpus and are registered for the follow-up study. Corrections for label error \citep{egami2023} would leave the simulated quantity unchanged in either case: they address disagreement between classifier and criterion, whereas the artefact here arises between platforms.
