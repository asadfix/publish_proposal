\begin{abstract}
Social media platforms are routinely pooled into single indices of public opinion, on the untested assumption that machine-labelled sentiment means the same thing on each platform. We treat each platform as a survey mode and apply the psychometric equivalence toolkit, with the platform itself as the grouping facet, to sentiment and emotion labels over UK economic policy discussions on \todo{N, recounted} posts across \todo{K} platforms. Around pre-identified fiscal events, anchored on the September 2022 mini-budget, we (i) establish that cross-platform divergence survives controls for topic and time, (ii) decompose it with a fixed-facet generalizability study against an expanded human-labelled criterion, (iii) test categorical-indicator measurement invariance and differential item functioning per event, (iv) link platforms where the evidence permits, and (v) quantify what a naive pooled index gets wrong about the timing and severity of public reaction. Where comparison is not justified, we say so; that verdict is a finding. The result is a reusable diagnostic for any study that aggregates machine-labelled text across platforms.
\end{abstract}
