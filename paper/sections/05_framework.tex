\section{Steps 2-4: Reliability, Invariance, Adjustment}
\label{sec:framework}

We treat the classifier as a measurement instrument and the platform as a candidate component of the measurement procedure \citep{jacobs2021, halterman2025}. The design proceeds in six steps: establish divergence (Section~\ref{sec:divergence}); estimate reliability per platform with a generalizability study; test measurement invariance and differential item functioning with platform as the grouping facet; adjust for composition and timing in a multilevel model; link scales where the evidence permits (Section~\ref{sec:linking}); and quantify the cost of skipping the first five steps (Section~\ref{sec:cost}). To our knowledge, this is the first application of the psychometric equivalence toolkit \citep{cronbach1972, meredith1993, brennan2001, kolen2014} to machine-classified policy sentiment with platform as the grouping facet.

Two commitments frame everything downstream. The anchor-density audit comes first: every event-window-platform cell must meet a minimum post count before it enters any model, and if cells fail, the study narrows to the dense subset of platforms rather than papering over sparsity \citep{morstatter2013}. And platform is a fixed facet throughout: our claims concern these four platforms, not a universe of platforms from which they were sampled.

\subsection{Step 2: Fixed-facet generalizability study}
The criterion is the gold-standard sample of Section~\ref{sec:data}. For each platform $m$ we estimate a generalizability study with posts nested in events and human coders crossed with posts, all facets random except platform:
\begin{equation}
X^{(m)}_{p(e)c} = \mu^{(m)} + \nu^{(m)}_{e} + \nu^{(m)}_{p(e)} + \nu^{(m)}_{c} + \nu^{(m)}_{ec} + \nu^{(m)}_{p(e)c,\varepsilon},
\label{eq:gstudy}
\end{equation}
with variance components for events, posts within events, coders, their interactions, and residual estimated separately at each fixed level $m$ \citep{cronbach1972, brennan2001}. Comparing components and dependability coefficients across platforms answers a question a single reliability coefficient cannot: whether the measurement procedure is equally dependable on every platform. Generalizability designs have recently modelled prompts and models as facets of automated annotation \citep{camuffo2026}, and annotator-facet studies exist \citep{kennedy2020, sachdeva2022}, but in all of them platform remains an unmodelled stratum.

\subsection{Step 3: Invariance and DIF with platform as the group}
We fit multiple-group measurement models with the machine-produced categorical codes, sentiment class and NRC emotion categories, as ordered-categorical indicators of latent policy sentiment, grouped by platform. Four safeguards discipline this step. Identification follows the threshold parameterisation for categorical indicators \citep{wu2016}; we do not treat categorical labels as linear indicators. Invariance decisions rest on effect-size criteria, changes in CFI and RMSEA \citep{chen2007, putnick2016}; chi-square is reported but never decides, because at this sample size it rejects trivial differences. DIF is tested separately within each anchor event, so an event-specific artifact cannot masquerade as a global platform effect. And because anchor choice can smuggle in the conclusion, we re-estimate DIF anchor-free \citep{chen2023} and run alignment optimisation \citep{muthen2018} alongside the constrained models; a claim of non-invariance must survive all three routes.

\subsection{Step 4: Multilevel adjustment}
Steps 2 and 3 could still confound measurement with composition. We therefore fit a multilevel model with posts nested in events and platform as a fixed effect. Time since event enters the model explicitly: platforms differ in reaction latency, and we treat that latency as part of the mode effect to be measured rather than noise to be trimmed, the same move the survey-mode literature makes for mode-specific response behaviour \citep{tourangeau2000, klausch2013, hox2015}.

\subsection{Classifier-artifact control and interpretive stance}
A platform effect estimated with one classifier could be an artifact of that classifier, and our training labels have a disclosed lexicon heritage. Every analysis is therefore replicated with a non-lexicon transformer, and only convergent non-invariance, the same pattern under both classifier families, is interpreted as a platform mode effect \citep{czarnek2022}. Divergent patterns are reported as classifier artifacts, which also constrains the analyst degrees of freedom that make automated-annotation pipelines fragile \citep{baumann2025, atreja2025}.

Finally, invariance evidence is diagnostic, not a licence \citep{robitzsch2023}. Passing a threshold does not certify pooled scores as interchangeable; it localises where comparability holds and where it breaks, and every claim in Sections~\ref{sec:linking} and~\ref{sec:cost} is conditioned on that localisation. The framework thereby formalises the platform-affordance error that \citet{sen2021} name but do not test, and reaches a failure mode that label-error corrections cannot, because those correct classifier error against a criterion within a platform, not non-equivalence between platforms \citep{egami2023}.
