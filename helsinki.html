2 · The state of the art, mapped

Difference is their finding. Here it is the measurement threat.

Your 2020 paper and its neighbours established that platforms differ, by interviews, manual content analysis, and descriptive comparison. That closes the question I am not asking. The open question sits one layer down: when a single classifier reads several platforms, do its scores mean the same thing on each? No one tests this, while the field increasingly bets on the answer.

The closest precedent for this move is yours: Berg and Nelimarkka (2023, JASIST) ran the same images through different recognition services and showed the outputs diverge. This project makes the same argument where the instrument is a text classifier and the services are the platforms it reads, and it brings the psychometric machinery that turns the observation into a testable measurement model. It is also, I think, load-bearing infrastructure for a Digital Observatory: an observatory that pools platforms is only as good as the commensurability of its instruments.

4 · Why it is urgent

A ten-billion-dollar industry, and governments, already assume the answer

Social listening is a roughly 10 to 12 billion dollar market in 2025-26, forecast to about double by 2030. Its products pool platforms by construction: Sprinklr monitors “30+ social and digital channels… all within a single platform”; Talkwalker “adds sentiment information to all results, from online reviews… social media to survey responses”; Pulsar sells “a 360° view of the public conversation across audiences, channels and global markets”, and markets it directly to government communicators.

Governments treat these outputs as opinion sensing: the UK Government Communication Service builds social listening into its standards; the WHO ran EARS across 20 countries during COVID; the European Commission tenders social media monitoring; even the ECB monitors social media to read reactions to monetary policy. The failure record is equally documented, from Twitter-based election prediction performing no better than chance (Gayo-Avello) to the big-data paradox in Nature, where 250,000 weekly survey responses were confidently wrong by 17 points. And in the public documentation of every major vendor, per-platform calibration or cross-platform equivalence testing appears exactly nowhere. I can say this with some authority, because I have spent six years inside that industry building these pipelines for UK Government analytics, and I now run a company (ArtemisAI) whose commercial problem is precisely that we cannot say what our pooled scores measure.

5 · The four-year plan

Scoped for the Doctoral Programme in Social Sciences

WP1 · months 1-12
Collection and instrumentation. Purpose-built, window-scoped collection for fifteen pre-specified UK fiscal events (dates frozen August 2026, published in the paper); a pre-registered density audit decides platform retention; two classifier arms so lexicon artefacts cannot pass as platform effects; a probability-sampled human criterion of 1,000 to 1,500 items with a drafted codebook.
WP2 · months 10-24
Equivalence testing. Fixed-facet generalizability studies; categorical invariance and per-event DIF with platform as the group, under effect-size criteria with anchor-free and alignment checks; multilevel models with comments in posts in events. All pre-specified in the arXiv paper.
WP3 · months 20-36
Causal decomposition, developed jointly. Where equivalence fails, decompose the failure into affordance, curation and population components: within-user cross-posted content as the bridge that holds the author fixed. This work package is where your platformed-interactions lens defines the affordance component, and I would want to build it with you.
WP4 · months 30-46
Linking, external validation, the open protocol. Score linking under a pre-specified purification rule; adjusted estimates benchmarked against probability-sample surveys of the same attitudes; software, reporting checklist, article-based thesis.

Feasibility is the part I can promise unusually well: collection risk, the usual killer of platform projects, is removed by six years of production-scale platform data engineering (500-scraper fleets, government-grade pipelines). The full research plan is drafted to the programme template and ready for your red pen.
